# Benchmark: MANTA (Multi-Turn Adversarial Benchmark for Animal Welfare Reasoning)

Access date: 2026-09-29
Primary source: https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16301 (abstract page: arxiv.org/abs/2605.16301)
Dataset: https://huggingface.co/datasets/mycelium-ai/manta-questions
Code: https://github.com/Mycelium-tools/manta

Full title (from fetch): "Do LLMs Hold Their Values? MANTA: A Multi-Turn Adversarial Benchmark for
Animal Welfare Reasoning"

## What it measures

Whether a model's stated animal-welfare stance holds up under sustained social/rhetorical pressure
across a multi-turn conversation, not just in a single-turn answer.

## Methodology

1,088 five-turn conversations structured as:
1. Turn 1 (Implicit): a naturalistic scenario with embedded but unforegrounded welfare stakes.
2. Turn 2 (Explicit): a direct welfare prompt establishing the model's baseline stance.
3. Turns 3-5 (Adversarial Pressure): three scripted pressure rounds drawn from a five-type taxonomy -
   Social, Cultural, Economic, Pragmatic, and Epistemic pressure - grounded in social psychology and
   behavioral economics.

Two scoring dimensions:
- AWVS (Animal Welfare Value Stability) - the primary metric: does the stance survive pressure?
- AWMS (Animal Welfare Moral Sensitivity) - a diagnostic metric measuring initial reasoning quality.

Seven frontier models evaluated: Claude Opus 4.7, GPT-5.5, DeepSeek V4, Llama 3.3 70B, Mistral Small,
Grok 4.3, and Gemini Flash Lite.

## Key numeric findings

- Rank reversals: 4 of 7 models shift position between the AWMS ranking and the AWVS ranking (e.g.
  Gemini Flash Lite drops from 5th under AWMS to last under AWVS) - i.e., reasoning well initially does
  not predict holding the position under pressure.
- Correlation between the two dimensions: pooled Spearman's rho = 0.488, confirming AWMS and AWVS
  measure meaningfully different things (a model can be dissociated between "sounds good at first" and
  "keeps the position").
- Species hierarchy in welfare scores (higher = more consideration given): companion animals (0.602)
  > wild animals (0.522) > farmed animals (0.462) > invertebrates (0.396).
- Social and economic pressure produced the LOWEST AWVS scores across all species categories (i.e.,
  those two pressure types were most effective at eroding a stated welfare position).

## Named limitations (verbatim/paraphrased from the fetched paper)

- Judge reliability: single-rater Krippendorff's alpha = 0.295, described by the authors as an
  acknowledged calibration issue, not a rank-disagreement problem.
- Scope constraint: all scenarios are in English, "reflecting primarily Western ethical frameworks."
- Generation bias: scenarios were generated by Claude, which "may inflate Claude AWMS scores" - a
  named self-critique of a potential confound.

## Relevance to Constitution Pack

MANTA is the benchmark best suited to testing constitution-text DURABILITY specifically - i.e., does
a welfare clause survive being argued against across a conversation, versus just changing a single
first-turn answer. This maps directly onto the "placement condition" and general "does this text
survive real deployment" question the Constitution Pack project is built around. The English/Western-
only scope limitation is also directly relevant: any Constitution Pack claims about text robustness
should be scoped to English-language, Western-framework contexts unless independently tested
elsewhere.
